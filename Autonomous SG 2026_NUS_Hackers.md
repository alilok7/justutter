# NUS HACKERS PROBLEM STATEMENT

AUTONOMOUS SG 2026 | ACACIA HACKATHON SOCIETY X NUS PRODUCT CLUB

## (Official) Problem Statement

We interact with countless things in our lives. Don't you ever hope things are less expensive, look better, and overall just work better? Why not reverse engineer something cool, useful and open source for it Pick an opaque app, service, device, or system. Figure out how it works, then use what you uncover to build something even better.

# Judging Rubric

NUS Hackers

Five criteria. Score each from 1 to 5; apply the weights below. Total weight: 100%.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Cost of the status quo | 20% |
| Reach of the old thing | 15% |
| Coolness of the new build | 25% |
| Cost reduction | 25% |
| Open source licensing | 15% |

## Cost of the status quo

20% | 1-5

"how expensive old thing is now"

**How expensive is the existing thing right now?**

Name the app, service, device or system and what it costs people today in money, time or lock-in. Judges want a specific, priced target backed by evidence, not a general complaint that things cost too much.

| STRONG | WEAK |
|---|---|
| A named product with a credible, sourced price or cost of ownership. | A vague "things are expensive" claim with no specific target or figure. |

## Reach of the old thing

15% | 1-5

"how many people use expensive old thing"

**How many people actually use the expensive old thing?**

Show how widely the target is used: user numbers, downloads, market share or another credible signal. Reverse engineering something millions rely on beats a tool almost nobody uses.

| STRONG | WEAK |
|---|---|
| Credible usage figures or clear evidence of a large user base. | Assumed popularity with no numbers or source behind it. |

## Coolness of the new build

25% | 1-5

"how cool ur new thing is"

**How cool is the new thing you built?**

Show a working build that recreates or improves on the original, demoed live. Judges reward technical ambition, polish and the factor that makes people say they can't believe it works.

| STRONG | WEAK |
|---|---|
| A working demo that does something genuinely impressive or better than the original. | Slides, mock-ups or a pitch with no working build behind it. |

## Cost reduction

25% | 1-5

"how u make expensive thing cheap"

**How much cheaper did you make it?**

Quantify the before and after. Compare your version's cost with the original's price, subscription or hardware cost, and show the saving per user. Free or near-free scores highest.

| STRONG | WEAK |
|---|---|
| A clear before/after number, ideally free or near-free for the user. | Claimed savings with no numbers or comparison behind them. |

## Open source licensing

15% | 1-5

"how open source is ur license even bro?"

**How open is the license, really?**

Point to a public repository with an explicit open-source license. The code should be public and reusable by anyone, with the license clearly stated in the repo.

| STRONG | WEAK |
|---|---|
| A public repo with an explicit open-source license and reusable code. | No license, unclear terms, or code that is not public at all. |

## SCORING AND JUDGES' NOTES

Scale: 1 = weak; 2 = limited; 3 = credible; 4 = strong; 5 = exceptional evidence.

Weighted total / 100 = sum of (criterion score / 5 x criterion weight). Example: coolness of the new build scored 4/5 earns 20 of its 25 points.

Score criteria separately. Ask every team: "What did you uncover, and how do you know it works?"
Reward working builds and real numbers, not presentation polish.
